The Story / Since 2016

Every Lure Earns Its Teeth Marks

Tideline Tackle Co. started at a bait cutting table behind a charter dock, not in a design studio. The paint was chipping off every stock plug we threw at redfish and the hooks kept folding on the first big hit. So we started pouring our own. Ten years and a few thousand hulls later, the vise hasn't stopped running.

From Deckhand To Lure Maker

01 / Origin

Ten seasons running charters out of Wilmington taught us exactly where store-bought tackle falls apart: cheap split rings, dull paint, hooks that were never meant for a 40-inch red. We started building the lures we wished existed.

02 / Bench

Every blank is hand-poured, sanded, and airbrushed in a one-room shop off the harbor. No overseas molds, no bulk runs. If a color batch looks off under the shop lights, it gets scrapped, not shipped.

What We Won't Cut

  • 01

    Saltwater-Grade Hardware

    Stainless split rings, forged trebles, no exceptions. A lure that rusts shut on the second trip isn't a lure, it's a paperweight.

  • 02

    Hand-Mixed Paint

    Every neon chartreuse and glow pattern is mixed in small batches so it holds up against sun, salt, and a bluefish's teeth.

  • 03

    Real Water Testing

    No tank tests. Every batch goes out on the surf or the inlet before it's cleared to sell. If it doesn't swim right, it doesn't go out.

  • 04

    Small Batch Runs

    We cap runs at what one person can pour and paint by hand. It means we sell out. It also means nothing leaves the shop half-finished.
