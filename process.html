Start to Finish

Six Steps, Zero Shortcuts

Every lure that leaves our shop goes through the same six stages. No shortcuts, no outsourced pours. Here's what happens before a bait ever touches the water.

  1. 01

    Blank Carving

    We start with kiln-dried balsa and poplar, hand-shaped on the belt sander to a profile we've tuned over hundreds of casts. Each blank gets checked for grain and weight before it moves on.

  2. 02

    Rigging & Ballast

    Stainless through-wire, brass ballast, and heavy split rings go in before any finish work. This is the step that decides whether a lure swims true or rolls out in a rip current.

  3. 03

    Sealing the Blank

    Three coats of sanding sealer, hand-sanded between each, so the wood won't waterlog after a season of bluefish and Spanish mackerel chewing on it.

  4. 04

    Base Coat & Foil

    Airbrushed base color goes down first, then prismatic foil is laid by hand for the scale pattern. This is the slowest step in the shop and the one we won't rush.

  5. 05

    Detail & Clear Coat

    Gill plates, spots, and eyes get painted freehand under a loupe, then the whole lure gets dipped in a marine-grade epoxy that shrugs off toothy fish and dock rash.

  6. 06

    Tank Test

    Before anything ships, it swims in our test tank at three retrieve speeds. If it doesn't run straight, it doesn't leave the shop. Simple as that.

What Goes In

Materials We Trust

We buy from the same suppliers year after year because switching to save a few cents means a softer blank or a foil that fades by June. Here's what's actually in the lure.

A

Kiln-Dried Balsa

Light, buoyant, and consistent grain, sourced from a single mill so every blank sands the same.

B

Marine Poplar

Denser than balsa, used in our sinking and deep-diving bodies for casting distance and durability.

C

Stainless Through-Wire

One continuous wire from nose to tail hook. It's the reason our lures survive a big red drum without opening up.

D

Prismatic Foil

Hand-laid scale foil under the clear coat throws flash at depth, even on overcast surf days.

E

2X Saltwater Hooks

Nothing goes out on a fresh-water hook. We rig heavy from the start so you don't lose fish at the boat.

F

Marine Epoxy Coat

A UV-stable finish that resists yellowing and holds up to teeth, piers, and rod tips for seasons at a time.
